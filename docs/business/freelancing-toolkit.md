## 📝 Proposal Templates

### Template A: Web / Software Development

```
Subject: [Project Name] — Proposal from [Your Name]

Hi [Client Name],

Thanks for sharing the details on [brief project description]. I've read through
your requirements and here's my understanding and proposed approach:

**Understanding:**
[2–3 sentences restating their problem in your own words — proves you read the brief]

**Approach:**
1. [Phase 1 — e.g., Discovery & wireframes — X days]
2. [Phase 2 — e.g., Core development — X days]
3. [Phase 3 — e.g., Testing & delivery — X days]

**Deliverables:**
- [List specific outputs: repo, deployed app, documentation, etc.]

**Timeline:** [X] weeks from kickoff
**Investment:** $[amount] [fixed / per milestone]

**Why me:**
- [Relevant experience or similar project]
- [Key differentiator: speed, tech stack expertise, communication style]

**Next step:** Happy to jump on a 15-min call to clarify scope. When works for you?

Best,
[Your Name]
[Portfolio URL]
```

## 👤 Profile Optimization Checklist

Use this for Upwork, Fiverr, LinkedIn, or any freelance platform.

### Headline (most important line)
- [ ] States WHAT you do + WHO you help (not just "Freelance Developer")
- [ ] Includes 1–2 keywords clients search for
- [ ] Example: "Full-Stack Python Developer | APIs & Automation for SaaS Startups"

### Bio / Overview
- [ ] First sentence hooks with a result or value prop (not "I am a…")
- [ ] Mentions specific technologies / skills (searchable)
- [ ] Includes a quantified result if possible ("cut load time 60%")
- [ ] Addresses the client directly ("You need X — I deliver X")
- [ ] Ends with a CTA ("Message me to discuss your project")
- [ ] 150–300 words (scannable, not a wall of text)

### Portfolio / Work Samples
- [ ] 3–6 pieces minimum (quality > quantity)
- [ ] Each has: screenshot/demo, 1-sentence description, result/outcome
- [ ] Matches the type of work you want MORE of (not everything you've ever done)
- [ ] Most impressive / relevant piece is FIRST

### Social Proof
- [ ] At least 3 reviews/testimonials (ask past clients explicitly)
- [ ] Certifications or tests completed (platform-specific)
- [ ] Badges: Top Rated, Rising Talent, etc. (work toward these)

### Platform-Specific Tips

**Upwork:**
- [ ] Specialized profiles (up to 2) for different niches
- [ ] Availability badge ON
- [ ] Connects budget: spend only on well-matched jobs
- [ ] Proposal first line references something SPECIFIC from their job post

**Fiverr:**
- [ ] 3 gig tiers (Basic / Standard / Premium) with clear scope differences
- [ ] Gig video (even simple screen recording + voiceover boosts conversions)
- [ ] Response time < 2 hours during business hours
- [ ] FAQ section answers the top 3 buyer objections

**LinkedIn:**
- [ ] Banner image with value prop text
- [ ] Featured section with case studies or portfolio links
- [ ] "Open to Work" (visible to recruiters) OR "Providing Services" badge
- [ ] Post 2–3x/week about your expertise (builds inbound)

## 💵 Rate-Setting Guide

### Calculate your floor (minimum viable rate)

```
Monthly costs (rent + food + bills + tools + taxes + savings buffer)
÷ Billable hours available per month (realistic: 60–80% of work hours)
= Hourly floor

Example: $3,000 / 100 billable hours = $30/hr floor
```

### Position by experience level

| Level | Hourly range (USD, general) | Positioning |
|-------|---------------------------|-------------|
| Beginner (0–1 yr) | $15–40 | Compete on speed, communication, reliability |
| Intermediate (1–3 yr) | $40–80 | Compete on quality, process, partial specialization |
| Expert (3+ yr) | $80–200+ | Compete on outcomes, niche expertise, reputation |

*Ranges vary wildly by niche, geography, and platform. Research YOUR market.*

### Value-based pricing (the goal)

```
What's the project worth to the client?
÷ 3–10 (you capture 10–33% of the value you create)
= Your project fee

Example: Client's new landing page will generate $50K in sales
         Your fee: $5,000–$15,000 (not $500 for "a web page")
```

### Rate structures

| Structure | When to use | Pros | Cons |
|-----------|-------------|------|------|
| **Hourly** | Unclear scope, ongoing support | Fair if scope creeps | Punishes efficiency; client watches clock |
| **Fixed/project** | Clear scope, defined deliverables | Rewards speed; client knows cost upfront | Scope creep risk; must define boundaries |
| **Retainer** | Ongoing relationship, priority access | Predictable income; client loyalty | Can feel like employment; underpricing risk |
| **Value-based** | High-impact, measurable outcomes | Highest earning potential | Requires confidence + proof of value |

### When to raise rates
- After 3+ successful projects with strong reviews
- When you're booked 80%+ of capacity (demand > supply = raise price)
- When you add a new skill/certification that increases value
- Annually, minimum (inflation + experience growth)
- For new clients (grandfather existing clients, or give 30-day notice)

### Red flags (walk away or charge premium)
- "We'll pay you in exposure / equity / future work"
- Scope described as "quick" or "simple" without details
- Client has no budget defined and won't discuss pricing
- Unrealistic deadline with no rush fee discussed
- Client fired 3+ freelancers on this same project
